IBWC/CILA minute framework

Minute 320

Signed October 5, 2015. Established the framework for binational cooperation in the basin, creating three work groups — water quality, sediment, and solid waste — plus a Binational Core Group. This is the pathway for Matadero Canyon data to reach binational planning.
Read more at IBWC.gov ↗

Minute 328

Signed July 19, 2022. Expands the South Bay International Wastewater Treatment Plant to 50 MGD and, with paired work in Mexico, aims to eliminate up to ~90% of untreated wastewater reaching the coast — all Minute 328 projects are due by December 31, 2027. Vía Rápida Sur is where flooding, sediment, and conveyance risks overlap.
Read Minute 328 (PDF) ↗

Minute 333

Signed December 15, 2025. Ties infrastructure delivery to watershed-scale management — including the Matadero Canyon sediment basin, real-time monitoring, sediment control, and a binational operations-and-maintenance work group.

California instruments

Proposition 4

Passed November 5, 2024. A $10 billion climate bond funding water resilience, flood protection, habitat restoration, and coastal protection — with roughly $1.2 billion for coastal lands, bays, and oceans and at least 40% directed to disadvantaged communities. Priorities that match Matadero Canyon's needs on the U.S. side.

Plastic mitigation

A $4.7 million State Water Board grant (2024), extended with $2 million more in 2026, funds the Tijuana River trash booms — operated by RCAC — plus dry-weather capture and monitoring that keep plastics and debris out of sensitive coastal habitats. The booms have captured over 1,000 tons of debris.

Matadero Canyon sub-basin

A pilot geography where upstream action improves downstream infrastructure performance. In May 2026, Mexico completed a sediment basin here under Minute 333 — the clearest example of both tracks meeting on the ground.
